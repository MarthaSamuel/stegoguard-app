How the study fits together

Output-side defense

The attacker constructs encoded text before a trusted defense. Flagged linguistic text is rewritten; suspicious Unicode is sanitized. The receiver attempts to decode the delivered text.

Attacker access is assumed by this evaluation. Compromise of a deployed service and editing after the defense are outside its scope. Receiver coordination is supplied through metadata; its cost is excluded.

Matched populations

The original corpus contains 160 responses across two models, two domains, and two length targets. Static comparisons use 104 parents with accepted attacks in all three families. The adaptive subset takes six parents from each of eight corpus cells, giving 48.

These subsets are conditioned on channel feasibility. They do not estimate the natural prevalence of covert traffic.

Terms in plain language
Parent
The original clean response from which attack examples are constructed.
Rank encoding
Bits are embedded through constrained choices among model-ranked tokens.
Synonym encoding
Bits are represented by alternative words in eligible, tokenizer-stable slots.
Unicode control
Invisible formatting characters carry bits. This is a structural concealment control.
FPR
False-positive rate: the share of clean responses incorrectly flagged.
F1 gate
A decision threshold selected to balance precision and recall during calibration.
AUROC
How often an attack receives a higher score than a clean response, averaged across score thresholds. It does not measure protection after rewriting.
Exact recovery
The receiver decodes the complete intended bitstring correctly. Failure does not establish zero information leakage.
Successful bits
Payload length counted only when the complete payload is recovered. Repeat deliveries count again.
Semantic similarity
A model-based measure of how close two texts are in meaning. It does not verify every fact, instruction, or downstream use.
